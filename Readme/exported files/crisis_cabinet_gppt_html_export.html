Step 2: Problem & Population

Problem Statement
Many IT project management students understand risk management theory but struggle to apply it in real project scenarios. Studies and industry reports often show that more than 60% of IT projects face budget, schedule, or scope problems because risks are identified too late or handled with the wrong response strategy. Crisis Cabinet solves this by giving students an interactive simulation where they classify risks by probability and impact, then choose suitable PMBOK-based responses.
Target Population
Third-year Computer Engineering students taking COM0463, approximately 40–60 students.
Success Criteria
  • At least 80% of players correctly classify risks as Tiger, Alligator, Puppy, or Kitten by the end of the game.
  • At least 75% of players choose the correct risk response strategy in final scenarios.
  • At least 70% of players complete the project without reaching 0% Contingency HP.
  • At least 70% of players complete the project without running out of budget.
  • Average post-game quiz scores improve by at least 20% compared with the pre-game quiz.

Step 6: Narrative & Concept Mapping

Narrative Theme
Crisis Cabinet places the learner in the role of a project manager leading a critical IT delivery through a tense cabinet-room simulation. Every meeting reveals a new risk animal from the Tusler Matrix: Tigers, Alligators, Puppies, and Kittens become memorable metaphors for probability-impact analysis. The story begins in Planning, where uncertain requirements and vendor choices are still preventable, then escalates into Executing and Monitoring, where developer resignations, security vulnerabilities, flaky tests, and stakeholder change requests threaten budget, schedule, and contingency reserves. The design uses Self-Determination Theory by giving autonomy in classification and response choices, competence through immediate feedback and failure audits, and relatedness through realistic stakeholders, developers, clients, and managers. It applies Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics by connecting scenario cards, resource bars, streaks, and audits to strategic tension, responsibility, and mastery. Bloom Taxonomy is embedded from recall of risk animals to creation of a new risk response plan. HEXAD player types shape rewards for achievers, social learners, and reflective players; Octalysis supports accomplishment, avoidance of project failure, ownership of resources, and unpredictability of crises. The Fogg Behavior Model supports simple decisions at the exact crisis moment, Flow Theory balances difficulty across project phases, Bartle-style achiever and explorer motivations support progression, Kirkpatrick four-level evaluation measures reaction, learning, behaviour transfer, and project-level results, and Tusler risk logic gives the game its central professional framework.
Game Overview
Crisis Cabinet is a single-player educational simulation for COM0463 IT Project Management. The player manages a software project from Planning to Closing while classifying risk scenarios using the Tusler Matrix and selecting a PMBOK-style response: mitigation, avoidance, transfer, or acceptance. Correct decisions protect Contingency HP, budget, schedule progress, and streak score. Wrong decisions reduce resources and trigger a failure audit that explains the violated risk management principle.

Step 12: Preview & Export

In Scope (compassion model anchor)
The project includes a digital single-player educational simulation game; Tusler Matrix classification; PMBOK-style response choices; Planning, Executing, Monitoring, and Closing phases; Contingency HP, budget, schedule, score, streak, and technical debt systems; 12 scenario cards; immediate feedback; failure audit; final report; anonymous learning logs; and a short pre/post assessment. The classroom compassion model is in scope: losePoints=0 for course grading, no punitive academic points are removed for failed runs, and failure becomes a structured learning audit.
Out of Scope
The project does not include multiplayer competition, real financial transactions, collection of sensitive personal data, or grading students negatively for failed simulation attempts. It also excludes full enterprise deployment, advanced AI scenario generation, and integration with university LMS systems in the first prototype.
Why (Motivation)
Crisis Cabinet motivates students by turning abstract IT project risk management into a meaningful project survival challenge. Autonomy comes from choosing classifications and responses; competence comes from feedback, streaks, and audits; relatedness comes from realistic project stakeholders and team consequences.
