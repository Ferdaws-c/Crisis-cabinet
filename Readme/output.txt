⚠  Three Things to Know Before You Start





What Is the Gamification Project Proposal Tool?
The Gamification Project Proposal Tool (GPPT) is a structured, 12-step browser-based wizard that guides you through producing a complete, formally assessed gamification project proposal. It replaces blank-page proposal writing with an interactive environment that gives you immediate score feedback, dimension-level guidance, and a navigable map of every design decision you need to make.



How to Use the Tool — Step by Step
Open the file gppt_integrated_v51.html in any modern browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari). No server, no install, no login required. The tool runs entirely in your browser.

First Session
Click "Start Building" on the welcome screen to begin a new proposal.
Work through Steps 1–11 in order. Each step shows which scoring dimensions it affects (see the colored badges at the top of each step).
Use the dimension bars in the sidebar to monitor your score as you work. Click any dimension bar to see score tips and navigate to contributing steps.
Complete Step 12 (Game Summary) manually — it does not auto-fill.
Click Export JSON and save the file before closing your browser.

Returning Sessions
Open gppt_integrated_v51.html in your browser.
Click Import JSON on the welcome screen and select your saved file.
Review the import preview and click Approve to reload your work.
Continue from where you left off.

Exporting Your Final Proposal
When your proposal is complete, go to Step 12 and export in your required format:
HTML — a self-contained file with a sidebar navigation, suitable for digital submission
Word (.docx) — for LMS upload or printed submission
Markdown — for version-control systems or plain-text submission
JSON — for re-importing your work into GPPT in a future session
Export your final proposal as both HTML (or Word) AND JSON. Keep the JSON as your working backup.

The 12 Steps — What You Fill In

Steps 4 and 11 are the most critical — together they cover D1 (Pedagogical Alignment), D2 (Game-Learning Integration), D3 (Motivational Design), and D4 (Assessment & Evaluation).
Step 10 is the only step that contributes to D7 (Risk Management). Do not skip it.


The 8 Scoring Dimensions
Your proposal is scored across 8 dimensions drawn from the GACT and G4E gamification assessment frameworks. Each dimension is scored from 0–100. Your overall score is the unweighted average of all 8 dimensions.

Technical Notes
File size: approximately 2 MB. Works offline once downloaded. No server connection required.
Supported browsers: Chrome 110+, Firefox 110+, Edge 110+, Safari 16+.
Mobile: functional but the layout is optimised for laptop/desktop screens.
The tool uses your browser's local storage to auto-save. If you clear browser data between sessions, you will lose unsaved work. Always export JSON.
Accessibility: the tool uses standard HTML form elements and is navigable by keyboard.


Note to Instructors / Teaching Assistants
Please share the following information with students before they first use the tool:


Assessment use
The GPPT score is a formative quality indicator only. It is not a direct course grade. Instructors should use the exported HTML or Word proposal as the primary submission artefact. The score and dimension breakdown inside the exported document can be used as supporting evidence during marking.

Originality check
The JSON export includes an originality score and the closest matching example. A similarity score above 65% triggers an alert in the tool and is flagged in the export. Instructors can use this as a first-pass similarity check, supplemented by standard academic integrity procedures.
